# THE ODD SOCKS

## Game Specification — v12.0

**Archived verbatim 2026-07-29.** This is the authoritative rules document referenced by
`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-07-29-odd-socks-v12-prototype-design.md`. Prior versions of this
project repeatedly stalled because the rules document lived outside the repository; it now does
not. Do not edit this file to record design changes — supersede it with a new version.

**Platform:** Steam, PC first
**Genre:** Silent social deduction · asymmetric horror · party game
**Players:** Exactly 6 at launch
**Session length:** 12–18 minutes
**Business model:** Premium game with Friend Pass
**Camera:** Stylised top-down / elevated storybook view
**Primary mode:** Online private-party multiplayer

> **Nobody speaks. The house remembers.**

---

# 1. High concept

Six children wake inside a haunted house.

Five are ordinary children. One is secretly **the Odd Sock**.

Every night the children explore darkening rooms, place lanterns to watch doorways, recover
abandoned socks and read the traces the house leaves behind.

The Odd Sock moves among them — putting out lights, moving evidence to where it does not belong,
and taking children who wander alone.

Players cannot rely on conversation. They communicate through movement, pointing, lantern
placement, a small glyph vocabulary, and a public claim board that records what each of them
asserted and when.

To win, the children must recover enough evidence to make a scarce accusation called **the Call**.
A correct Call reveals the Odd Sock but does not end the match. It begins **the Last Night** — a
played final hunt in which the survivors must trap the revealed villain using light.

---

# 2. What changed from v11, and why

Auditable list. Everything else in v11 stands.

| # | Change | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The Plant is cut.** Fake socks no longer exist. Replaced by **Displace** (§12.3) | The fake needed five supporting systems to work and fired once per match. Its detection test was arithmetic across nights — unplayable at real-time speed |
| 2 | **The Call names a person only.** No sock designation | Follows from 1. Removes the case where correct deduction still loses on a coin flip |
| 3 | **Six separate starting rooms; no Take on Night One** (§8) | v11's three-rooms-of-two handed the Odd Sock a 1-on-1 immediately, contradicting its own sub-10% first-night target |
| 4 | **Carrying a sock blocks your special action** (§11.4) | Gives Displace a real price and stops the Odd Sock hoarding evidence for free |
| 5 | **A Shed sock lands in the Odd Sock's actual room** (§12.4) | v11 dropped it vaguely "on that floor." With only two floors that leaked almost nothing, so hiding all six nights was too cheap — and dawn is a villain win |
| 6 | **The morning is built on the claim board, not the glyph chain** (§13) | Glyphs do not survive Discord. A committed public claim does |
| 7 | **The Bind is guaranteed reachable** (§16) | v11's minimum configuration could be structurally unavailable exactly when the children were weakest |
| 8 | **Timing corrected** (§5) | v11's phase table summed to ~15 minutes against a stated 16–22 |
| 9 | **A test bot moves into Prototype 1** (§29) | v11's first prototype could not be evaluated without six networked humans |
| 10 | Dawn after Night Six remains an Odd Sock win | Confirmed, not changed. §5 and §12.4 compensate |

# 3. Design pillars

**3.1 Silence creates meaning.** The limited communication system must produce interpretation,
convention and misunderstanding — not merely the absence of talking.

**3.2 Information is physical.** Evidence exists in the house. It must be found, carried,
protected and risked for.

**3.3 The game never lies.** The referee never invents false readings. Darkness may hide.
Reports may be incomplete. Nothing the house says is untrue.

**3.4 Every hostile act leaves evidence.** Taking, snuffing, moving a sock and using the passage
all produce an observable consequence. The evidence may be partial. It is always honest.

**3.5 Accusation is scarce.** Calls cost recovered evidence, and failing one materially worsens
the children's position.

**3.6 Elimination changes your role, not your stake.** Taken children stay on the children's team
and keep playing.

**3.7 The ending is played.** Correct deduction launches a climax. The game never ends on a menu
announcing that a guess was right.

# 4. Player structure

## 4.1 Count

Exactly six: five children, one Odd Sock. No player-count selector at launch.

Other counts require their own maps, thresholds and balance work, and may only be added after
the six-player game is proven.

## 4.2 Identity

Each player controls a visually distinct child. The Odd Sock has no visible tell before being
successfully Called and can perform every ordinary child action.

## 4.3 Shared victory

Taken children remain on the children's team. When the children win, the result screen tells
every taken player, in plain words:

> **The children escaped. You won.**

---

# 5. Match structure

Up to six normal nights, then a possible Last Night.

**Flow:** opening → night → dawn → house report → silent morning → optional Call → next night →
Last Night on a correct Call → personal postmortem → haunting reel → rematch prompt.

## Timing

| Phase | Duration |
|---|---|
| Opening | 30s |
| Night | 90s × up to 6 |
| Morning | 60s × up to 6 |
| Last Night | up to 90s |
| Post-game | 45–60s |

A full six-night match runs **~18 minutes**. A match ending early — three takes by Night Four —
runs **~12**. Both numbers include the post-game.

Session length feeds directly into how many concurrent players you need for a lobby to fill, so
these are stated as arithmetic rather than aspiration.

# 6. The house

## 6.1 Layout

Eight active rooms drawn from a larger pool, across two floors, plus the Shared Bedroom and the
Hearth Room. Two stair connections. One conditional secret passage.

Room pool: Nursery, Kitchen, Library, Bathroom, Music Room, Attic, Playroom, Conservatory,
Cellar.

Connections may vary between matches, but the full floor plan is shown before play begins.
Players should be uncertain about **who moved where** — never about the architecture.

**No room has more than two exits**, counting doorways and stairs. This is a hard constraint,
not a preference: the Bind (§16.3) requires sealing every exit, and a three-exit room would be
untrappable.

## 6.2 Room readability

Every room needs a distinctive silhouette, a unique ambient sound, clearly visible exits, one
recognisable central object, a readable light state, and limited hiding geometry.

Darkness may conceal identity and detail. It must never obscure navigation.

---

# 7. The Hearth

Five flames in the Hearth Room represent the house's remaining safety. They are the Odd Sock's
pressure track and the match's public clock.

**A flame goes out when:**

- The Odd Sock Takes a child
- The Odd Sock Snuffs a communal lantern
- The children make a failed Call
- A publicly telegraphed house event resolves against the children

**The Odd Sock wins immediately when:**

- All five flames are out, or
- Three children have been taken, or
- Dawn arrives after Night Six with no successful Bind

**The children win only by a correct Call followed by a successful Bind.** Survival alone is not
a win condition — this is what stops huddling from being a strategy.

Note the structure: the Odd Sock's two active routes both burn flames, and **every flame burned
hands the children a sock.** Acting is how the villain wins and how the villain arms the people
hunting them. That tension is the engine of the whole design; nothing should be added that lets
either side advance without feeding the other.

---

# 8. Opening and Night One

All six children appear to wake together in the Shared Bedroom. The house then separates them
into **six different starting rooms** — one player each, randomised.

**No Take is possible on Night One.** The Odd Sock may move, observe, place lanterns and search,
but cannot act hostilely until Night Two.

Both halves of this matter. Separate rooms remove the opening collision. The Night One grace
removes elimination-by-first-blind-guess, which is the single least defensible way to lose a
game of this kind — the victim has no information with which to have chosen differently.

The Odd Sock's inactivity counter (§12.4) does not run on Night One.

---

# 9. Night exploration

Living players move through the house in real time.

**Every player can:** walk, run, open and close doors, point, carry a sock, carry a lantern,
place a lantern, search a room, recover a house object, return to bed, follow another child, and
hide briefly behind furniture.

The Odd Sock can do all of it without restriction.

## Identity in darkness

In deep darkness: names disappear, pyjama colours desaturate, faces lose detail, silhouettes
partially obscure. Footsteps and movement remain perceptible.

**A player being Taken does not receive reliable confirmation of who took them.** This is what
makes the game survive external voice chat — the one fact that would end the match cannot be
transmitted, because nobody has it.

---

# 10. Lanterns

Two communal lanterns. They are safe zones, surveillance devices, strategic objectives and the
tool required for the final Bind.

## 10.1 Carrying

A carried lantern gives weak personal light but does not protect the room. While carrying it:
you cannot carry a sock, it records no door traffic, you move slightly slower, and your light is
visible from nearby doorways.

## 10.2 Placement

A placed lantern illuminates a defined area, **prevents Takes inside its radius**, records
movement through one doorway of the placer's choosing, speeds searches in its light, and becomes
vulnerable to Snuffing.

## 10.3 Dawn record

A surviving lantern reports how many living figures crossed, in which direction, roughly when,
whether movement was calm or hurried, and whether the lantern was moved.

It never reports names.

> **Music Room Lantern**
> Two figures entered. One left hurriedly.
> The lantern was not moved.

## 10.4 Snuffing

The Odd Sock may Snuff a placed lantern when no living child is inside its radius. A Snuff puts
out a flame, disables the lantern, produces a genuine sock nearby, leaves soot, preserves the
lantern's final doorway record, and makes a sound audible in adjacent rooms.

## 10.5 Relighting

A snuffed lantern can be relit with a Matchbox recovered from a dark room. Relighting takes
several uninterrupted seconds. The Matchbox is consumed.

---

# 11. Socks and evidence

Socks are the only currency that buys an accusation. Everything about them should be simple
enough to hold in your head while running.

## 11.1 Where socks come from

Three events, all genuine. There are no fake socks in v12.

| Event | Flame goes out? |
|---|---|
| The Odd Sock Takes a child | Yes |
| The Odd Sock Snuffs a lantern | Yes |
| The Odd Sock Sheds after two quiet nights | No |

## 11.2 What a sock records

Room where it was **found**, night it appeared, pattern detail, and whether a lantern observed
movement nearby.

It does not record where it originated, and it does not identify who produced it. That gap is
the villain's lie (§12.3).

## 11.3 Securing

A sock on the floor is worth nothing. It must be carried to the Shared Bedroom and placed on the
incident board to count. Once secured it is permanent and public.

## 11.4 Carrying

While carrying a sock:

- You cannot hold a lantern
- Your footsteps are louder
- The Odd Sock receives occasional vague directional awareness of you
- **You cannot use your special action** — for the Odd Sock this means no Take, no Snuff, no Slip
- You drop it if Taken, and anyone may pick it up
- The Odd Sock may not carry a sock into the Shared Bedroom, and must drop it before dawn

The last two lines are what make evidence contestable rather than a formality. Carrying is the
most dangerous thing a child does, and the most expensive thing the villain can do.

---

# 12. The Odd Sock

One hostile action per night. Take, Snuff, Displace or Slip — never two.

## 12.1 Take

Available when the room is dark enough, the target is outside lantern protection, no second
living child is close enough to intervene, and contact is maintained for the required duration.

**A Take is not instant.** The target gets a brief sensory warning and may run, reach lantern
light, fire one final context glyph, or be interrupted by someone entering.

A successful Take: turns the victim into a ghost, puts out a flame, creates a genuine sock in
that room, disturbs a nearby object, and produces a muffled sound audible **on that floor**.

The victim does not learn who took them.

## 12.2 Snuff

Puts out an unattended placed lantern. Costs a flame, creates a sock, leaves soot, disables the
lantern.

## 12.3 Displace

**The villain's lie.** The Odd Sock picks up an unsecured sock and leaves it in a different
room.

Costs the night's action. Costs no flame. The sock records where it was **found**, not where it
fell — so the evidence now points at the wrong room.

Two things make this readable rather than arbitrary. A Take produces a sound on its floor
(§12.1), so moving a sock between floors is detectable by anyone who was listening. And while
carrying, the Odd Sock cannot Take (§11.4) — so a night spent moving evidence is a night the
children are safe.

This replaces the Plant. The villain no longer lies about whether evidence is real. They lie
about **where it happened**, which is a claim other players can contradict from what they saw
and heard, in the moment, without arithmetic.

## 12.4 Shed — the anti-hiding rule

The Odd Sock cannot win by behaving like an ordinary child for six nights.

- After **one** quiet night, the house reports which floor the Odd Sock ended on
- After **two consecutive** quiet nights, a genuine sock is Shed **into the exact room the Odd
  Sock ended the night in**, with an audible disturbance, and the counter resets

Displace and Slip both count as quiet nights. Only Take and Snuff reset the counter.

The Shed landing in the true room is deliberate and load-bearing. Dawn after Night Six is a
villain win, so hiding is a genuine strategy — and it has to have a price sharp enough that
sitting still for six nights loses. A vague floor report across a two-floor house was not that
price.

## 12.5 Slip

Once per match, the Odd Sock may use the secret passage between two eligible rooms. It makes a
house-wide pipe sound, leaves a subtle disturbance at one endpoint, and cannot be used while
carrying a sock or lantern.

---

# 13. Morning

Every living player returns to bed. Ghosts gather invisibly. The house presents a public
incident board.

## 13.1 The house report

The house reports facts and never interprets. It reads out, every morning, in order:

- Which children did not return
- Flames lost, and how many remain
- Lantern records
- Socks secured
- Rooms disturbed
- Floor-level sounds
- Inactivity traces
- Ghost disturbances
- The announced Midnight room, when relevant

> The Nursery stayed dark. The house saw nothing.
> The Music Room lantern watched two figures enter and one leave.
> A child did not return. A sound came from upstairs.
> One flame went out. Four remain.

**Design rule:** every mechanic in the game must be learnable from these reports alone. If a
system cannot be taught by the house saying what happened, it does not belong in the launch
version.

**Voice rule:** the house's phrasing depends only on the room and the night, never on who was
standing in it.

## 13.2 The claim board

This is the centre of the morning, and it is deliberately the piece that survives players
talking on Discord.

Each living player may place **one claim marker per morning**. A claim links:

- Player → room
- Player → player
- Player → sock
- Room → incident

**Claims are permanent and public for the rest of the match.** They stack into a visible record
of who said what, on which night, about whom.

Speech is deniable. A placed claim is committed. When someone's night-two claim contradicts
their night-four claim, the board shows it whether or not anyone remembers saying it — and that
is the one deduction channel voice chat cannot erode.

The Odd Sock uses the same board and may lie freely.

## 13.3 Glyphs

The live in-night channel, and the flavour of the morning: **Me, You, Saw, Followed, Alone,
Together, Sock, Danger, Unsure.** Chainable.

> Me → Saw → You → Attic
> Sock → Nursery → Unsure

Glyphs are for pointing, warning and gesturing in the moment. They are not asked to carry
complex arguments, because they cannot — the claim board does that job.

---

# 14. Ghosts

## 14.1 What ghosts see

Ghosts may move through the house, watch general movement, see room occupancy, lantern states,
and socks on the floor.

Ghosts may **not** see the Odd Sock's identity, read their private actions, identify living
players in deep darkness, carry objects, or make a Call.

## 14.2 Whisper

One per night. A Whisper may make a lantern flicker, send a draft through a doorway, disturb a
small object, leave a partial glyph, or reveal whether a chosen room was occupied.

The signal is visible to whoever is nearby and always incomplete. The Odd Sock can tell that
ghost activity happened, but not always what it meant.

## 14.3 Ghost vote

Each ghost keeps **one** Call vote for the rest of the match. Once spent, it is gone.

## 14.4 Called shot

The moment you are taken, you privately name who you think the Odd Sock is. It does not affect
the match.

At the end you learn whether you were right, what you actually knew at that moment, and what was
really happening in your final three rooms.

This gives a taken player an unresolved personal outcome to follow for the rest of the match —
you are not rooting to have survived, you are rooting to have been right, and you were uniquely
placed to judge.

---

# 15. The Call

## 15.1 Unlocking

**Two secured socks unlock one Call.** Both are consumed. A match normally supports one or two.

## 15.2 Contents

The group names **one living child** as the Odd Sock. That is the entire claim.

## 15.3 Voting

Every living player votes, including the accused and including the Odd Sock. Each ghost may
spend their single remaining vote. A strict majority of votes cast carries.

## 15.4 Failed Call

Destroys both socks, puts out a flame, and reveals only that the name was wrong.

**It does not clear the accused.** A wrongly-named player gains no protection and no status —
they simply survive, still suspected. Protect this in writing: it is the reason there is no
execution vote in this game, and it removes the entire class of "get yourself accused early to
be cleared" play that most games in the genre have to live with.

## 15.5 Correct Call

Reveals the Odd Sock, changes their visible socks, stops the night cycle, and begins the Last
Night immediately.

Correct identification is the gateway to the climax, not the victory.

---

# 16. The Last Night

Up to 90 seconds. The house visibly collapses.

## 16.1 Transformation

The Odd Sock's mismatched socks become visible. Their silhouette distorts. The Hearth burns
fast, doors slam, peripheral rooms begin sealing, and every remaining player spawns into an
active position rather than a bed.

## 16.2 Tracking

The Odd Sock is known but not permanently visible. At fixed intervals the house reveals one
trace: previous room, direction of travel, an odd footprint, a moving shadow, a recently used
doorway.

Traces come faster as the timer runs down.

## 16.3 The Bind

To Bind, the children must hold the Odd Sock in one room for **four seconds** with **both exits
sealed** and at least one living child keeping sight of them.

An exit is sealed by a placed lit lantern, or by a living child holding the doorway.
**At least one exit must be sealed by a lantern.**

Three rules guarantee this configuration always exists, which v11 did not:

1. **No room has more than two exits** (§6.1)
2. **Lanterns cannot be permanently killed during the Last Night.** The Odd Sock may knock one
   out, but it relights after ten seconds
3. **The collapse seals doorways progressively** (§16.5), so late in the Last Night, rooms
   become dead ends

A Bind must be hard. It must never be impossible because of which lantern died on Night Three.

## 16.4 Odd Sock actions

Sprint briefly, slam one door, knock out a lantern with a longer interruptible action, use one
enhanced Slip, push past a single doorway holder after a charge-up, create one false directional
shadow.

## 16.5 Collapse

A peripheral room seals every fixed interval. The collapse narrows routes, prevents endless
looping, makes prediction increasingly accurate, and forces both sides toward the Hearth.

## 16.6 Outcomes

**Children win** when the Bind completes.

**Odd Sock wins** when the timer expires, all flames go out, they reach and corrupt the Hearth,
or three children have been taken in total.

---

# 17. House objects

Two neutral objects per match. Anyone may use them, including the Odd Sock.

- **Keyhole Mirror** — briefly shows movement in an adjacent room without opening the door
- **Music Box** — momentarily reveals every moving silhouette in its room; audible nearby
- **White Rabbit** — creates a short-lived false movement silhouette
- **Matchbox** — relights a snuffed lantern

All single-use, immediately understandable, publicly recognisable, and never tied to permanent
character abilities. This is how the game gets variation without role bloat.

---

# 18. Pacing

Fixed, readable escalation. No hidden adaptive director.

**Nights One and Two** — most rooms keep faint ambient visibility. Players establish routes and
lantern positions. No Take is possible on Night One.

**Night Three** — ambient light drops. Dark searches take longer. Footsteps matter more.

**Night Four: Midnight** — before the night begins, the house names one room. At Midnight, every
silhouette inside it is briefly revealed publicly. Converge for evidence, avoid it, use it to
back a claim, or exploit it.

**Nights Five and Six** — peripheral routes close, relighting becomes valuable, inactivity
traces sharpen, the house report gets shorter and harsher, and the pressure to Call rises.

---

# 19. Anti-turtling

The game does not punish grouping with arbitrary damage. It makes grouping unproductive:

- Socks must be physically recovered from dark rooms
- Placed lanterns only watch areas nobody is standing in
- The Odd Sock can Snuff lanterns away from the group
- Calls require evidence
- **The children cannot win by surviving**
- The Odd Sock Sheds evidence — in their true room — if prevented from acting
- Dawn after Night Six is a villain win

Grouping is temporarily safe and permanently losing.

---

# 20. Sound

Sound carries mechanical information. Players must learn to distinguish bare feet, slippers,
running, careful walking, doors on each floor, lantern sputtering, fabric dragging, a nearby
Take, a Snuff, ghost drafts, passage movement, the Music Box, and the Hearth collapsing.

The soundtrack is restrained. Music rises mainly during sock recovery, a Call, Midnight, the
Last Night, and the haunting reel.

---

# 21. Art direction

## Characters

Big heads, small bodies, roughly 1:3. Eyes only, no mouths. Strong silhouettes, flat pyjama
colours, bare feet or loose socks, dark brown-plum outlines, restrained texture, expressive
posture.

Ten launch child models, each identifiable in silhouette and in darkness.

## House

Hand-drawn storybook architecture. Uneven scale and perspective, oversized furniture, warm pools
of lantern light against cold muted surroundings, shadows that feel subtly alive, rooms that
seem familiar but wrongly proportioned.

Not realistic horror. **A children's picture book becoming hostile.**

## Animation priorities

Nervous eye movement, hesitant doors, dragged blankets, scuffing slippers, freezing when
observed, comedic collisions, sudden pulls into darkness, lantern reactions, ghost disturbances.

Comedy comes from physical behaviour, never dialogue.

---

# 22. Post-game

## 22.1 Personal postmortem

Every player first gets their own reconstruction: their last three rooms, what they could and
could not see, evidence they walked past, whether their claims held up, how close they came to
the Odd Sock, and their called-shot result if taken.

## 22.2 Full timeline

Then the whole match, inspectable three ways: what the group publicly knew, what any one player
knew, and what actually happened.

## 22.3 Haunting reel

Automatically selected moments — a close encounter, a misread ghost warning, a displaced sock
being secured, a contradiction on the claim board, a failed Take, a correct suspicion that got
voted down, the decisive Last Night.

Launch version may be a stylised in-engine sequence rather than edited video. Clip export is
desirable, not required.

---

# 23. First session

**Tutorial** — a five-minute playable dream teaching movement, light and darkness, lantern
placement, sock recovery, glyphs, the claim board, the Call, Whisper and the Bind. It should not
read like a manual.

**First match assistance** — clearer lantern reports, longer Take warning, slower collapse,
suggested glyph combinations, strong visual explanation of a failed Call, guided post-game.

Most rules should be understandable from the house report by the end of one match.

---

# 24. Accessibility

Full controller support, remappable controls, patterns as well as colours for pyjamas, visual
sound indicators, subtitles for all house sounds, adjustable darkness intensity, high-contrast
interactables, reduced distortion, reduced camera shake, simplified glyph selection,
hold/toggle options, text descriptions of lantern reports, colour-independent Last Night
indicators.

**The game must be fully playable without voice chat.**

---

# 25. Social and lobby

## 25.1 Default

Private room codes are the primary flow. Public matchmaking is secondary and is not the launch
promise.

## 25.2 Friend Pass

One owner hosts; up to five invited players join free. Friend Pass players cannot host, cannot
enter public matchmaking alone, and receive no permanent cosmetic progression — but play full
private matches.

Six players means five separate humans each clearing an entry barrier. Friction does not add
across a lobby, it multiplies.

## 25.3 Queue discipline

One ruleset. One player count. No ranked queue, no skill-based matchmaking, no regional mode
selection unless technically forced.

Concentrating the audience matters more than offering choices.

---

# 26. Progression and monetisation

Retention comes from the group, the incidents and the reveal. Progression is secondary.

**Earnable:** pyjama colours, slippers, bed decorations, lantern designs, ghost effects, profile
frames, bedroom toys.

**Paid cosmetics:** themed pyjama sets, bed frames, nightlight designs, additional child
silhouettes, house-decoration themes.

**Never:** loot boxes, pay-to-win, random paid rewards, paid gameplay information, expiring
battle passes, FOMO countdowns, unlockable stronger roles.

---

# 27. Launch content

One highly polished house · multiple room layouts · ten child silhouettes · two communal
lanterns · four house objects · nine glyphs · the claim board · one Odd Sock role · ghost play ·
sock evidence · Calls · the Last Night · personal postmortems · full timeline · basic haunting
reel · private room codes · Friend Pass · reconnection · tutorial · cosmetic rewards.

One exceptional house beats several shallow ones.

---

# 28. Explicitly deferred

Additional villain roles · character abilities or skill trees · secret personal objectives ·
adaptive AI director · any system that generates false information · ranked · multiple player
counts · sophisticated deduction bots · battle pass · daily streaks · user-generated maps ·
narrative campaign · lore archive · multiple houses · audience voting · esports · persistent
stat advantages.

Reconsider only after the base match generates strong organic replay.

## Prototype 1 — is the silence fun?

Placeholder art. Six seats, eight-room fixed map, real-time movement, darkness, two lanterns,
Take, Snuff, Displace, socks, morning report, claim board, pointing and glyphs, one Call, basic
Last Night, ghost Whisper.

**Plus a simple test bot that can fill a seat.** Not a shipped feature — a way to evaluate the
prototype without assembling six networked humans every time. v11 deferred bots to §28 while
requiring six players to test anything, which meant the first real signal would arrive only after
most of the game was built.

The bot never plays the Odd Sock in any session that matters. It cannot defend itself in a
morning, and a villain who cannot argue is a different villain.

No progression. No final art.

## Prototype 2 — is the evidence good?

Lantern doorway records, the full claim board with permanent history, two possible Calls, Shed,
personal postmortem, basic complete timeline.

## Prototype 3 — is it a product?

Final art, sound language, house objects, tutorial, Friend Pass, haunting reel, accessibility,
reconnection, network polish.

# 30. Gates and kill conditions

## Behavioural targets

- Most groups ask for an immediate rematch
- Players discuss specific incidents afterwards, not just who won
- Calls rest on evidence rather than vibes
- Taken players keep paying attention
- Huddling does not dominate
- The Odd Sock has to act rather than wait
- Players understand why a Call failed
- The Last Night feels connected to the investigation
- The reveal regularly surprises
- New players understand the core after one match

## Quantitative targets

**These are guesses, not measurements.** Nothing in this document has been derived from play of
this ruleset, and no number below should later be cited as though it were.

| Target | Value |
|---|---|
| Immediate rematch rate, organised group | above 60% |
| Last Night reached | 60–75% of matches |
| First Call by end of Night Five | most matches |
| Calls per match | 1–2 |
| Children win rate | 45–55% |
| First-night Take rate | **0% — guaranteed by §8** |
| Last Night duration | 40–75s |
| Taken-player inactive time | effectively zero |
| Dominant lantern-placement pattern | none |

## Kill conditions

Cut or redesign a mechanic when:

- Players cannot explain it after two games
- It produces information but no decisions
- It generates waiting rather than tension
- It creates outcomes that feel arbitrary
- It exists to solve retention before the base game is replayable
- It weakens the chain of movement → light → evidence → Call → Bind
- **It needs more than one supporting rule to function.** This is what removed the Plant, and it
  is the cheapest filter in this document

# 31. Steam positioning

## Hook

Children are asleep inside a house that remembers every footstep.

Each night you wake and explore its darkening rooms. Place lanterns, recover abandoned socks,
and watch what your friends do when they think nobody can see them.

One child is wearing odd socks.

Communicate without words. Risk everything on a single accusation. Then survive the Last Night
long enough to trap what you uncovered.

## Features

Silent social deduction for six · real-time exploration of a hand-drawn haunted house ·
light-based surveillance · physical evidence that can be moved and misread · taken players

# 32. Open questions

Honest list. These are unresolved, not oversights.

**1. Is the silence fun?** The premise of the entire project, and the one thing no specification
can answer. Everything else here is re-tunable. This is not.

**2. Is two socks the right price for a Call?** With three sock sources and six nights, the
children may reach two socks by Night Three routinely — or never, if the Odd Sock spends nights
displacing. The number is a guess.

**3. Does Displace get used?** It costs the villain their whole night and their offence. It may
turn out that Taking is simply always better, in which case the villain's lie collapses back to
lying on the claim board — which might be enough, or might leave the villain with nothing to do
on quiet nights.

**4. Is dawn-after-six too kind to hiding?** §12.4's Shed is the counterweight and it has never
been tested against a villain who genuinely commits to sitting still.

**5. Does the claim board actually carry arguments?** It is asked to do the work glyphs cannot.
If players find it too slow to use inside a 60-second morning, the morning has no working
deduction channel at all.

**6. Can a group coordinate a Bind without speech?** Four seconds, both exits, one lantern, one
watcher. That is a co-ordination problem in a game with no words, under a 90-second timer.

**7. What does the Odd Sock actually do on the four nights they aren't taking anyone?** The seat
is one in six of all play and its moment-to-moment experience is the least examined thing in this
document.
