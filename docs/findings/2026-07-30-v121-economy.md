# ODD SOCKS v12.1 — second adversarial pass, by measurement

Date: 2026-07-30. Target: `docs/rules-v12.1.md`.
Method: exhaustive DFS over every legal villain action line across Nights 1–6
(`2026-07-30-v121-economy.py`, 282–402 lines depending on the object draw). No sampling,
no policy bot, no number in this file that the script did not print.

This is the **second** pass. The first (`2026-07-30-v12-design-review.md`) argued from clauses.
v12.1 fixed nearly all of it. This pass exists because that review ended with one item held back:

> **⚠️ Held back deliberately — do not apply blind.** Pricing grouping in §12.1 … A rule that
> changes where or whether evidence lands … should be **measured before applying — not reasoned
> about further.**

**v12.1 §14 is that rule, applied.** So it is the first thing measured here, and it is the lead
defect. Everything below is about the *economy* — flames, socks, the Call clock — which is pure
enumeration and needs no engine. Nothing here touches continuous space, so nothing here is blocked
by the missing prototype.

---

## 1. Verified strengths — read before the defects

- **The engine in §2 holds.** With same-night securing, **0 of 402 and 0 of 282 legal villain lines**
  deny the children a Call — and that holds *even with carrier interdiction switched on*. "Acting arms
  you" is true: there is no villain line that starves the board by action choice alone. This reproduces
  the prior review's supply floor of exactly 2 from a different direction. Everything that goes wrong
  below is a rule left unpinned or a rule newly added, not the engine.
- **The passive villain is not a stall.** All-quiet sheds on Nights 2, 4 and 6.
- **The B1 fix works.** Lanterns relighting for the Last Night removes the double-snuff kill
  entirely; snuffs now cost the villain a flame and a sock for a lantern the children get back.
- **A wrong Call is survivable as intended.** One sock and one flame, and the flame arithmetic in
  §3 below shows the sock is the cheaper half.

---

## 2. BLOCKER — §14 lets the *villain* fire the children's anti-huddling penalty

§14 prices "three or more children in one room for a night." The rule never says who has to put
them there, and **the villain is one of the six bodies.**

**The count must be over bodies, not over genuine children.** This is forced, not chosen. §8's
morning report includes "whether the children crowded together," and §8's voice rule is absolute:
*"The house never says anything that depends on who someone is."* If the count excluded the
villain, then a room holding the villain and two children reports **no crowd** — and those two
children, who watched three bodies stand there all night and know each other, have just been told
the third one was the Odd Sock. That is a direct identity reveal, and it is precisely the failure
§8 exists to prevent. So the villain's body counts.

**And walking into a room is not a hostile action.** §7 is explicit: *"One hostile thing per night.
Never two,"* and its list is grab / snuff / passage. Movement is on §4's everyone-can-do-it list.

⇒ **The villain burns one flame per night, for free, by stepping into any room holding two
children — and still takes its hostile action elsewhere the same night.**

Measured, same villain line set, the only change being §14 firing once a night from Night 2:

```
 crowd/night  villain wins  earliest Call  flames donated
         off       night 4             M2               0
     from N2       night 3             M2               2
```

The villain's fastest win moves **Night 4 → Night 3**, and the children's earliest possible Call is
M2. They get **one** morning, once, with a two-sock board and nothing else, or they lose.

### Both readings of the trigger fail

The defect is not the magnitude, it is that no reading survives:

| Reading | What breaks |
|---|---|
| **Any 3-body co-presence** | The free flame above. The children cannot refuse — §4 removes faces, so they cannot see who walked in, and cannot leave a room they don't know has been joined. |
| **End-of-night occupancy** (what "spend a night" / "for a night" actually says) | The children huddle for eighty-five seconds and scatter for the last five. §14 never fires. The free-crowding equilibrium §14 was written to kill is restored intact. |

The second reading is the one the text supports, and it is the one where the rule does nothing.
The partial defence of the second reading is that §7's grab needs "about three seconds of contact,"
which fits inside a five-second scatter — so scattering is not quite free. It is close enough to
free to not carry the rule.

This is the same failure shape as v12.0 §8.0: a timing pin that decides the game, presented as
wording. It is also the same *kind* of defect the first pass on this project ever found — a rule
whose stated purpose is inverted by its own implementation. §14's purpose is to punish the children
for grouping. As written, it is a villain ability.

### The obvious counter, priced

The crowd-join is not information-free, and the counter deserves stating before someone raises it.
§8's morning report *does* tell everyone a crowd happened, and the prior review's roster-continuity
result (pairs agreed awake in the lit bedroom, committable to the board) means a joined pair knows
an intruder arrived even if it cannot see who. Across three roster pairs, the other two pairs can
account for their own members, so the intruder is identifiable **by elimination**.

So the villain pays for each crowd-join. It pays roughly one bit against a **five**-flame clock,
while the children spend a mark and a morning to collect it. That is not a rescue — it is a bad
exchange rate that the villain can afford four times. But it is the one thing making the ability
less than free, and it should be priced in whatever replaces §14.

### It also refutes §16 Q3 on paper

§16 Q3 asks whether two pairs under two lanterns breaks the game and says *"No flames burn."* Under
the co-presence reading, the villain walks into a lantern room, makes it three bodies, and burns
one. The turtle formation is grab-proof and snuff-proof and still loses a flame a night, at the
villain's discretion. Q3's premise is wrong; the answer is that the turtle bleeds.

## 3. BLOCKER — retrieval latency is unpinned, and it decides the race

§6 says find a sock, carry it to the bedroom, put it on the board. It never says whether a sock that
appears during Night *n* can be on the board at morning *n*, or only at *n+1*. One unstated word.

A "denying line" below is a villain line after which the children **never** reach two secured socks
before the villain wins — no Call, no Bind, no path to their only win condition. A villain picks its
line, so **one denying line is enough**; the counts measure how much room the villain has, not a
probability.

```
 matchbox  latency  interdict  lines  denying  exists?  best Call
     True        0      False    402        0       no         M2
     True        0       True    402        0       no         M2
     True        1      False    402        6      YES         M3
     True        1       True    402       22      YES         M3
    False        0      False    282        0       no         M2
    False        0       True    282        0       no         M2
    False        1      False    282        6      YES         M3
    False        1       True    282       21      YES         M3

  shortest denying exemplars (matchbox=False, latency=1):
    QU GR GR GR QU QU  -> villain wins night 4, board never reaches 2
```

`QU GR GR GR` is the defect in four moves: grab on Nights 2, 3 and 4, win by the take clock on Night
4, with the board stuck at **one** sock of the two a Call costs. Traced explicitly: N2's grab creates
the first sock (nothing existed to interdict yet), N3's grab drops that sock's carrier, N4's grab is
the third take. Board = 1, needed 2.

**Latency is the whole switch.** At latency 0 the count is **0 in every column** — the children always
get their Call. At latency 1 it is 6 before the villain does anything clever. Pin it explicitly in §6,
as a rule and not as flavour. Latency 0 is the reading that keeps the children alive, and it is the
harder one to build: a sock created mid-night must be findable and deliverable inside the same ninety
seconds.

## 4. MAJOR — carrier interdiction amplifies §3, and its sanctioned counter backfires

§6 makes a grabbed carrier drop the sock, and says so approvingly: *"an unclaimed sock on the floor is
the best ambush spot in the house."* So a grab can take a child *and* keep the board short.

**It is an amplifier, never sufficient alone** — the table above shows 0 denying lines at latency 0
whether interdiction is on or off. At latency 1 it takes 6 → 21. That is what demotes this from the
BLOCKER I first graded it: interdiction cannot deny a Call by itself, it can only widen a hole latency
already opened.

§7 supplies the counter: a grab needs *"no second child close enough to interrupt,"* so an **escorted**
carrier is grab-proof. I expected that to close the denying lines. **It does not — it quadruples
them**, because the villain's answer to an escort pair is §2's crowd-join:

```
               defence  denying lines  flames donated  earliest Call
    unescorted carrier             21               0             M3
      escorted carrier             96               3             M3
```

This refuted my own prior and is why this pass was run as a script rather than an argument. The flames
an escort donates are worth more to the villain than the interdiction it prevents. Both child options
lose, and the rules recommend the worse one.

Note the coupling: §2, §3 and §4 are not three independent defects. **The crowd-join is the villain's
answer to every child formation** — escort a carrier, get crowd-joined; huddle for safety, get
crowd-joined; split up, get grabbed; turtle under lanterns, get crowd-joined. §14 promises "there's no
free way to be safe." It delivers something else: no way to be safe at all.

## 5. Which unpinned rule actually decides it

The cross-sweep, because the three interact and the ranking is not what I expected going in:

```
 latency  crowd-join  interdict  lines  denying  best Call
       0         off      False    282        0         M2
       0         off       True    282        0         M2
       0     from N2      False    282       28         M2
       0     from N2       True    282       28         M2
       1         off      False    282        6         M3
       1         off       True    282       21         M3
       1     from N2      False    282      227         M3
       1     from N2       True    282      227         M3
```

Three things fall out, and they set the delta order:

1. **§14's crowd-join dominates everything.** It alone moves 0 → 28 in the children's *best* corner
   and 6 → 227 (80% of all legal lines) in the worst.
2. **Once the crowd-join exists, interdiction stops mattering at all** — 28 == 28 and 227 == 227. The
   flame clock fires before the sock economy gets a chance to. Any fix aimed at interdiction while
   §14 stands is treating a symptom that is not currently load-bearing.
3. **Fix §14 and pin latency to 0, and Call-denial disappears entirely: 0 of 282 lines.** Two changes,
   one of them a single sentence, close the whole class.

## 6. MAJOR — the Matchbox is a trap, and §5 recommends it

```
  Matchbox drawn = True  -> flame ceiling = 5/5   (children relit once -> 3rd snuff target)
  Matchbox drawn = False -> flame ceiling = 4/5   (no relight available)
```

The label matters: this is the ceiling reachable **by villain action, given what the children do with
the Matchbox** — the relight is a child's choice, not the villain's. With no relight the villain's own
actions cap at **four** flames: two grabs (a third wins by the take clock instead) and two snuffs (two
lanterns exist). The children must donate the fifth. So the flame clock is mostly a self-destruct
mechanism, which reframes §16 Q7 from "is the crowd rule too harsh" to **"the crowd rule is
load-bearing for the villain's second win condition"** — and §2 above shows the villain, not the
children, decides when it fires.

Relighting a lantern restores a snuff target and lifts the ceiling to exactly five. §5 says *"You
can relight it with a Matchbox if you find one"* without noting that doing so hands over the flame
that completes the loss. The only counter to a snuff completes the villain's win.

---

## 7. MINOR — §16 Q2 is off by one, in the children's favour

Q2: *"A completely passive villain produces two, which is exactly one accusation."*

The shed fires on Nights **2, 4 and 6** — three socks. A Call is payable at M4, and because M6 is a
live Call window (§10 lets any living child start one in any morning; §2's dawn loss comes *after*
Night Six), the third sock funds a **second** attempt. Combined with §10's wrong-Call cost of one
sock, a passive villain gives the children two swings, not one. Q2's tension is real but it is
looser than stated.

## 8. Recommended v12.2 delta order

Sequenced by §5's measured dominance, not by severity label. Items 1 and 2 together take Call-denial
to zero; item 3 may then need nothing at all.

1. **Price the crowd rule against the villain, not the room.** §14's trigger must be something the
   villain cannot fire unilaterally and the children cannot dodge with a five-second scatter. The
   first pass held this back for measurement; it has now been measured, and the *applied* version is
   worse than the unpriced clause it replaced. Do not iterate on the trigger wording — the body-count
   requirement in §2 above is *forced* by §8's voice rule, so every occupancy-based trigger inherits
   it. **Price the children's behaviour through something only they control** (evidence rate, search
   speed, lantern budget), not through who is standing where. Whatever replaces it, keep §2's
   traceability exchange rate in view: the villain currently pays about one bit for one of five flames.
2. **Pin retrieval latency in §6.** One sentence — may a sock found on Night *n* be boarded at morning
   *n*? Latency 0 is the answer that keeps the children alive, and with item 1 it takes denying lines
   to 0 of 282. Note the build cost: the client must let a mid-night sock be found *and* delivered
   inside the same ninety seconds.
3. **Only then look at carrier interdiction**, and check whether it still needs a fix. §5 shows it is
   fully masked while §14 stands (28 == 28, 227 == 227) and it denies nothing on its own at latency 0.
   If it does need one: make a dropped sock stay secured-in-progress, or make an escorted carrier
   explicitly un-grabbable, or exempt the first sock. Do not spend a rule here before re-measuring —
   fixing 1 and 2 may leave nothing to fix.
4. **Note the Matchbox's cost in §5**, or cap snuffs at one per lantern per match.
5. **Correct §16 Q2** to three shed socks and two Calls, and **§16 Q3** to "the turtle bleeds a flame
   a night at the villain's discretion" rather than "no flames burn."

## 9. What this pass cannot answer

Unchanged and unchanged again: **is the silence fun** needs six humans, and one tester is still the
binding constraint. This pass produced no play data. Everything above is the flame/sock/Call
economy, which is countable; nothing above validates the premise, the Bind, or the board.

Also untouched here: whether the Bind is coordinable without words (§16 Q4), whether the board
carries an argument inside a one-minute morning (§16 Q5), and §16 Q6. Those need the prototype.

**One caution on the numbers.** The enumerator models nights at night-granularity and is generous to
the children everywhere it is uncertain: every ripe sock comes home, the children always Call correctly
the first time, and deduction is free. The denying-line counts are therefore **lower bounds on the
villain's advantage.** A model this generous to the children still finding `QU GR GR GR` is the
interesting kind of result.

One modelling error was caught and corrected mid-pass, and it changed a severity: the first version let
a grab interdict the sock that same grab had just created, which is impossible — nothing is on the floor
to carry yet. Guarding it took the latency-0 interdiction columns from 6 denying lines to **0**, which is
what demoted interdiction from BLOCKER to the amplifier in §4 and promoted the latency pin to §3. The
guard is in the script; the pre-guard numbers are not cited anywhere above.
